# Product plan template

Each section starts with a one-line guide to the decision it records. Replace the guide line as decisions are made. Sections marked *(optional)* can be dropped when they don't apply.

Conventions inside every section:
- Decision lines: plain bullets or short sentences.
- `Open:` list for undecided questions. Remove an entry once decided.
- `> **Advice:**` blocks for recommendations, kept apart from decisions.
- Links to supporting files as `path/to/file.md`, instead of copying their content.

```markdown
# <PRODUCT> product plan

## Target Market
<Who buys, in which tiers or segments, and what each segment needs. Which segment comes first. Define segments by one clear dividing line first (e.g. government vs private sector), then add details. Needs every segment shares go on one line, not under each segment. Assign borderline organisations explicitly.>

## Competitive Position
<Who the product is benchmarked against, and the one or two reasons a buyer picks it. Link to competitor research.>

## Product Model
<What the customer gets: capabilities, form factors, speed/capacity options. Core: what every customer gets. Add-ons: what is sold separately. Test for Product vs Business Model: if changing the price or payment terms would change the line, it belongs in Business Model.>

## Business Model
<How the customer pays and how the company earns: one-time, licence or subscription; tiers; support; add-on pricing. Per purchase model: what is bought once, what recurs, how updates arrive, what happens on lapse.>

## Hardware and IP (optional)
<What hardware is built or sourced, and where the proprietary value sits.>

## Certification Strategy (optional)
<Which certifications, for which buyers, what scope, cost, duration, and in what order.>

## Go-to-Market
<How the first customers are reached and how that extends to the next ones.>

## Regulatory Exposure (optional)
<Export control, data protection or sector rules that constrain sale or shipment, and what must be done before selling.>

## Strategic Fit
<What the product is for the company: product line, project, upsell; what investment it justifies.>

## Timeline
<Milestones by month or quarter, and the objectives they serve. Start with the goal (e.g. first PO) and a dated current status. Then one table: a row per month (with year), a column per track (e.g. commercial, development, certification). Development, testing and delivery are separate phases, each with a start and end. External durations (certification, procurement) as ranges, with the condition for the early date. Notes: how the goal date moves if a key condition fails.>
```
